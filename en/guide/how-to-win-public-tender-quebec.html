How to win a public tender in Québec: a guide for SMBs and the self-employed

Published October 12, 2026 · Updated October 12, 2026

Winning a Québec public tender starts with not being disqualified. A bid that misses a single compliance condition can be rejected even if it has the best price. Here are the steps, with the official requirements.

General information, not legal advice. Check the requirements in each tender document and with the official bodies.

1. Check your eligibility before bidding

  • RENA: a business listed in the register of enterprises ineligible for public contracts can't bid.
  • Revenu Québec certificate (attestation): required for contracts of $25,000 or more.
  • Francization (OQLF): businesses with 25 or more employees in Québec must comply with the francization process.
  • AMP authorization to contract: for construction contracts and subcontracts of $5M or more and services of $1M or more. See our AMP guide.
  • RBQ licence for construction. See NEQ and RBQ licence.
  • Employment equity program: commitment required for businesses with more than 100 employees bidding on supply or services contracts of $100,000 or more.
  • clicSÉQUR: an access code is needed throughout the process.

2. Read the notice and documents in full

Start with the notice: contract type, closing date and time, regions, trade agreements, compliance conditions, bid security. See how to read a SEAO notice. Then read the tender document: eligibility conditions apply to the business, compliance conditions to the bid.

3. Avoid automatic rejections

Per the Québec government, rejections include: - a bid submitted after the deadline or to the wrong place; - a required document not attached; - for paper bids, a document not signed by an authorized person.

Also attach the lobbying declaration form, required with every bid.

4. Understand how the contract will be awarded

The award method is stated in the tender document: - Lowest compliant price: supply, most services, construction, IT. - Minimum quality and price: lowest price among bids meeting minimum quality. - Quality-price: quality adjusts the price; lowest adjusted price wins. - Quality only: limited cases (e.g., advertising, travel). - Specific rules for architecture and engineering services related to construction. See engineering and architecture.

Write your bid around the stated criteria, in order and with the requested evidence.

5. Tips for SMBs and the self-employed

  • Target contracts at your scale: regionalized tenders are reserved for businesses in a region; below thresholds, a Québec department or agency should generally invite at least three businesses including at least one SMB (250 employees or fewer).
  • Self-employed: an individual operating under their own legal name (first and last name) generally doesn't need to register with the Registraire des entreprises; under any other name, registration is mandatory. Other requirements (Revenu Québec, compliance) still apply.
  • Subcontracting: bidding as a subcontractor to a larger firm is often a first step. Check which requirements also apply to subcontracts (AMP, RBQ).
  • Ask questions in time: notices often set a minimum lead time for information requests.
  • Don't miss notices: check SEAO and CanadaBuys daily, or let a monitoring service do it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I win a public tender in Québec?

First confirm eligibility (RENA, Revenu Québec certificate, AMP and RBQ where applicable), read the notice and tender document in full, avoid automatic rejection causes, then write your bid around the stated award method.

Can a small business win Québec public tenders?

Yes. Regionalized tenders and invitation-only tenders (generally at least three businesses including one SMB for departments and agencies) are common entry points.

Does a self-employed bidder need an NEQ?

An individual operating under their own legal name generally doesn't need to register; under another name, registration (and an NEQ) is mandatory. Check each tender's requirements too.

Why do bids get rejected?

Common causes: late or misdirected submission, a missing required document, or on paper, a document not signed by an authorized person.

Does the lowest price always win?

No. It depends on the stated method: lowest compliant price, minimum quality and price, quality-price, or quality only.
