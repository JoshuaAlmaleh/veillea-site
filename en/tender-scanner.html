What a tender scanner actually does

Public buyers in Canada don't publish in one place. Québec public bodies and municipalities post on SEAO, federal departments on CanadaBuys, Saskatchewan on SaskTenders, and some cities run their own portals. Each site has its own search, its own categories and its own notification settings.

A government tender scanner sits on top of those portals and does three jobs:

  1. Collects new notices from several sources on a schedule.
  2. Matches them against a profile you define: keywords, categories, regions.
  3. Alerts you, so you open one email instead of five websites.

It doesn't replace the official portal. You still download documents and submit your bid where the buyer requires it. The scanner's job is to make sure you see the opportunity in time to decide.

How Veillea scans tenders

Keyword filters in English and French

Notices from Québec are mostly in French; federal notices come in both languages. Add terms in both (for example "snow removal" and "déneigement") and Veillea matches either. You can run several profiles for different lines of business.

Sector and region filters

Narrow by category (UNSPSC/GSIN) and by the regions you actually serve, so a Gatineau contractor isn't flooded with Saguenay paving jobs. Browse examples by sector or by region.

Daily email alerts

Paid plans send matching notices by email every day, ranked by relevance, with an English/French summary of scope, buyer and deadline, so you can triage in minutes and only open the full notice when it's a fit.

Sources Veillea scans

Veillea currently scans 14 live sources. In Canada:

  • SEAO: Québec's official electronic tendering system (provincial bodies and municipalities)
  • CanadaBuys (AchatsCanada): federal government tenders
  • SaskTenders: Government of Saskatchewan and public sector
  • City of Toronto

Veillea also covers ten US sources, including SAM.gov and several state and city portals.

Veillea does not scan MERX. If MERX-only opportunities are important to you, see our MERX comparison.

Who a bid scanner is for

  • Small contractors and consultants who can't spend an hour a day searching portals.
  • Québec businesses that want SEAO and federal opportunities in one feed.
  • English-speaking firms bidding in Québec, who need French notices summarized in English.
  • Sales and bid teams sharing several profiles across product lines.

If you bid only once or twice a year on a single portal, the free plan or the portal's own alerts may be enough. A bid scanner pays off when you watch several sources or several sectors.
